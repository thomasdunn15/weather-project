# Polymarket probe sizing: 100 -> 150 contracts/signal

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Status:** live
**Files:** `scripts/live_trade_polymarket.py`, `scripts/reconcile_pm_trades.py`

## What changed

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| `CONTRACTS_PER_SIGNAL` | 100 | **150** |
| `DAILY_SPEND_CAP_CENTS` | $200 | **$300** |
| max signals/day | 2 | 2 (unchanged) |
| cumulative kill | -$300 | -$300 (unchanged) |

The spend cap moved with the size deliberately. At 2 signals x 150 x up to 95c
the old $200 cap would have bound before the size did, silently truncating the
second signal and making the change look ineffective rather than wrong.

## Evidence

Measured book depth on the side we actually consume, at the 14:47 decision time,
over the 20 forward-logged Miami PM signals. Buying either leg means hitting the
*opposite* side's bids and paying 100-p; reading the same-named side reports junk
1c levels as the touch, which is how a first pass at this produced nonsense.

| within | p10 | p25 | median | p75 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| touch | 6 | 29 | 74 | 127 |
| **+2c** | 33 | 158 | **265** | 799 |
| +5c | 33 | 279 | 631 | 1121 |

Fully fillable within the +2c allowance: 50 -> 85% of days, 100 -> 80%,
**150 -> 75%**, 200 -> 65%, 300 -> 40%.

150 keeps three quarters of days fully filled. The touch itself is thin
(median 74), so nearly all size comes from the 2c allowance — that allowance is
load-bearing, which is the main reason not to push past 150.

## The contrary datapoint — read this before sizing up again

The single real fill we have disagrees with the snapshot measurement:

- 2026-08-20 14:43 snapshot: **212 contracts** available inside the 52c bound
- 2026-08-20 14:47 execution: **56.3 filled** of 100 requested, at 47.9c

The book moved in our favour on price (47.9c vs the ~50.7c the snapshot implied)
but was far thinner on size. The snapshotter runs every 5 minutes, so these
depth percentiles describe the book up to 5 minutes stale — not what is takeable
at the instant an IOC lands.

One observation is not a pattern, but it is the only ground truth we have and it
points the wrong way: achieved fill was ~25% of snapshot depth. If the fill-rate
series now being recorded keeps landing near that ratio, **150 is already too
large and the correct move is down, not up.**

## Why this is now measurable

`scripts/reconcile_pm_trades.py` (new, cron 13:05 and 20:05) writes `fill_count`
and `fill_avg_price_cents`, so filled/requested becomes a daily series. Roughly
10 days of it replaces every percentile above with achieved fills.

## Latent bug fixed at the same time

`settle_unsettled()` in `live_trade_polymarket.py` reads `fill_count`, which only
the reconciler writes. An unreconciled row was indistinguishable from a genuine
no-fill, so a real filled trade would be stamped `settlement='no_fill'`,
`realized_pnl_cents=0` — and the cumulative kill switch sums exactly that column.
The -$300 stop could therefore never have fired.

It now waits 2 days before believing a zero, giving the twice-daily reconciler
time to land. No historical damage: PM was unfunded until 2026-08-20, so
`pm_live_trades` held exactly one row when this was found.

## Risk accepted

The kill switch is armed but has **never actually fired**. At 150 contracts a
run of bad days reaches -$300 faster than it would have at 100. That is accepted
deliberately: the probe's purpose is measuring fill quality, and the stop is now
wired to real data rather than to a column nothing wrote.

## Revisit

After ~10 days of achieved fill-rate data, or immediately if realized P&L
approaches -$200.
