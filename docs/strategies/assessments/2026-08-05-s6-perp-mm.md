Kalshi's public `/margin` endpoints provide free, unauthenticated access to orderbook, 1-min candles, trade history, and funding rates [doc:2026-08-05-kalshi-perpetuals:1]. No capital required to backtest—you can run a paper period immediately.

**Data sufficiency: ✗ Thin window**  
Funding history is available since 2026-06-03 (~2 months) [doc:2026-08-05-kalshi-perpetuals:7]. This covers only two summer market regimes and may not capture volatility/spread regimes that matter for MM edge.

**Edge plausibility: ✗ Steeply capacity-bound at quoted spreads**  
Kalshi BTC-perp maker fees are 5.0bps per side (~10bps round trip), with spreads quoted at 0.3–11bps and professional MMs already competing. To break even on a round-trip trade cycle, you need an edge of at least **10bps net of adverse selection**—not the 2–3bps I previously estimated. The sports-betting analogy (19.2% / +1.4% / −0.7%) does not transfer here; its fee structure (5% bookmaker commission) mismatches crypto maker-fee economics by ~4x. Any single-operator non-colocated edge in this regime faces a steeply unfavorable S/N ratio.

**No internal evidence to build on**  
Zero paper or live trade data for this market [strat:kalshi-btc-perp:noevidence]. Any edge hypothesis is external-only; a probe run (2–4 week paper trade) is required.

**Testability verdict: Probe is technically feasible but edge hypothesis is implausible before probe.** At 10bps round-trip breakeven against professional incumbent MMs in a 0.3–11bps spread regime, the capital and calendar cost of the probe should weigh heavily against priors on single-operator MM edge in tight-spread markets. A paper period is worth running *only if* you have a specific mechanism (e.g., micro-volatility prediction, latency-free order flow) that differentiates from existing MMs—not as a blind exploration.
